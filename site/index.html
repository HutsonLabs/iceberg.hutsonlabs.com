It links itself.

Wiki-links and backlinks are the floor. Above that, relations are typed. Supports, contradicts, cites, causes: the graph records not just that two things touch but how, and each type carries its own color from a palette validated for color-vision deficiency.

And it's yours to push around.

Grab any node and drag it. Turn a content type off and the graph re-reads without it. This is the same structure the app draws, with the same colors meaning the same things. The legend chips are the filters, in the app and here.

Then your agent reads the whole thing.

Iceberg ships a server for the Model Context Protocol, the standard way an agent connects to your tools. So an agent (Claude, in my case) works in the same notes, journal, and relations you do. Ask what connects two ideas and it answers out of your graph, not its training. Let it file what it learns and you can walk to exactly where it put things.

The server runs on your Mac, where any MCP client can launch it. iPhone and iPad carry the same graph, minus the agent hookup.

What it won't do.

The shortest way to say what this is, is to say what I refused to build into it.

  1. Require an agent.

    The notes, journal, and graph are a complete app on their own. Connect an agent when you want one, or never. Nothing is greyed out waiting for a key.

  2. Take your data.

    The settings pane says it plainly, and so will I: nothing here leaves your device today.

  3. Trap your notes.

    No format only Iceberg can read. What you wrote stays something you can walk out with.

  4. File anything invisibly.

    The agent's edits land in the same graph you see, typed the same way, where you can follow them back. No hidden index, no second memory you can't inspect.
